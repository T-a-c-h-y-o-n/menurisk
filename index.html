How the matching actually works

  • We compare ingredient names, product details, brands and categories — not a guess, and not a language model.
  • Matches come in levels: exact phrase, brand/firm match, synonym equivalent (rocket ↔ arugula), fuzzy similarity, and category-level.
  • Every match repeats the wording it matched on, so you can verify or dismiss it in seconds.
  • Weak or generic entries don't produce matches. "cheese" alone will not be matched against every cheese recall — we ask for a more specific name instead.
  • Nothing is presented as confirmed. A match tells you where to look, not what to conclude.

Risk levels you'll see

CRITICAL check today — a high-confidence match to an active Class I recall

HIGH review the same day — a confident match to a Class II recall

MEDIUM review when you can — lower confidence or category-level signal

Questions

What exactly does the scan check?

Your ingredient list against active US food recall records from the FDA openFDA food enforcement dataset. Each result shows the recall product, the recalling firm, the class, the reason, lot information when the agency published it, why it matched, and the source link.

How does the matching work?

Deterministic rules: exact phrase, brand or firm match, synonym equivalent (rocket → arugula), fuzzy similarity, and category-level. No language model decides your matches, and every match carries the wording it matched on.

How accurate is it?

Honest answer: matching is text-based. The FDA and USDA do not publish reliable UPC/GTIN data, so a match tells you where to look, not what to conclude. Generic entries like "cheese" are flagged rather than guessed, which keeps obviously wrong matches out.

What if a match is wrong?

Treat every result as a review task. Each match shows the wording it matched on, so a weak match can be dismissed in seconds — and your record shows you looked.

Does this replace our food safety procedures or our HACCP plan?

No. It monitors recall data against your ingredient list. Your procedures, your supplier checks and your HACCP plan stay yours, and they stay necessary.

Does it guarantee compliance?

No. It's a monitoring and record-keeping aid — not a compliance guarantee, not a certification, and not legal advice.

What data sources do you use?

FDA openFDA food enforcement data (public domain, US government work). USDA FSIS coverage is being added; until it is, this page says so plainly instead of implying it.

How often is the data checked?

A free scan is a one-time check on the data as of that moment, and the result states the date the data was pulled. Monitoring re-checks daily — that part isn't live yet.

How often are recalls updated?

The FDA refreshes the food enforcement dataset on the agency's schedule. Our pulls are timestamped, and the timestamp is shown in your results and your emails.

What about a recall that gets updated or resolved?

Status changes are tracked. A closed recall is shown as resolved and its risk is downgraded rather than deleted, so you can see the history instead of guessing what happened to it.

Can I use this for multiple locations?

Each scan covers one list. Monitoring plans (when they open) cover a single location, with a separate check per location. Multi-location support comes later.

What file formats can I upload?

CSV or plain text: UTF-8, up to 1 MB, up to 5,000 lines, one ingredient per line. A header row is optional, and there's a template you can download from the form.

Do I need technical knowledge?

No. Paste a list, read a result. Everything is in plain language, and the reason for each match is written out.

Is my menu data secure, and who can see it?

Your list is used to run your scan and, if you enable it, to monitor it. It isn't published and isn't sold or shared with third parties. We only make security statements we can stand behind — anything stronger than that isn't on this page yet.

Is this only for the US?

Yes, at launch. The data covers US recalls only, and we'd rather say that up front than let you assume otherwise.

Do I have to pay to see a result?

No. The scan and the result are free, and you keep the record. Paid monitoring is a separate, future thing — it's not being sold here until it works.
